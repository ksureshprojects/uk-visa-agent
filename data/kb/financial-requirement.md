# Standard Visitor Visa — Financial / Maintenance Requirement

## No fixed minimum amount

Unlike some other UK visa routes (e.g. Student, Skilled Worker), the Standard Visitor route has **no fixed minimum bank balance or published financial threshold**. There is no gov.uk-published "you must show £X" figure for this route. This remains confirmed (not a gap) after re-checking the live gov.uk guidance directly.

## What must instead be shown

The applicant must satisfy the decision maker that they:
- Can **support themselves and any dependants** for the whole of the planned visit, **or**
- Have **funding from someone else** (a sponsor/host) who will support them,

and either way, have access to sufficient resources to maintain and accommodate themselves adequately for the entire length of the planned stay, covering:
- Day-to-day living costs during the visit.
- Accommodation costs (unless being hosted for free, in which case this should be evidenced instead — see `required-documents.md`).
- The cost of the **return or onward journey**.
- Costs relating to any **dependants** travelling with them.
- The cost of any **planned paid activity**, e.g. private medical treatment, course fees for short study, etc.

## Without working or accessing public funds

Critically, the funds relied on must **not** come from working in the UK (which is prohibited — see `prohibited-activities.md`) or from UK public funds/benefits (also prohibited).

## What financial documents must show

gov.uk's guide to supporting documents states applicants need "financial documents showing that you have sufficient funds available. These must clearly show that you have access to the funds, such as: bank statements which detail the origin of the funds held; building society books which detail the origin of the funds held." The emphasis is on **origin of funds** — a statement/transaction history that shows the money is legitimately the applicant's own (salary, savings accumulation), not just a headline balance.

## Recency of financial evidence

The live guidance identifies as **less useful evidence**: "bank statements or letters issued more than 1 year before the date of application." This resolves the recency gap flagged in earlier KB versions: gov.uk does **not** publish a strict "must be within the last N months" rule, but it does explicitly caution against relying on statements/letters older than 1 year. Applicants should still be advised to use reasonably recent evidence, even though no exact cutoff (e.g. "6 months") is stated in the official guidance.

## Third-party sponsorship

If someone else (a UK-based friend, relative, or a host organisation) is funding or hosting the visit, the application should evidence:
- The relationship between the applicant and the sponsor/host.
- The sponsor/host's own ability to fund/host (e.g. their own bank statements, payslips, or proof of accommodation).
- A clear statement of exactly what costs the sponsor/host is covering (all costs vs partial).

## Judgment-based assessment

Because there is no published numeric threshold, the assessment is inherently case-by-case, based on the applicant's stated itinerary, length of stay, and accommodation arrangements. This knowledge base should not invent a specific number (e.g. "£X per day") — no such official figure exists for this route, and any third-party website quoting a specific minimum balance for the Standard Visitor visa should be treated as informal guidance, not an official gov.uk requirement.
