# Standard Visitor Visa — Eligibility Criteria

To be granted permission as a Standard Visitor, an applicant must satisfy the decision maker (Home Office caseworker) that all of the following apply:

1. They will **leave the UK at the end of their visit**.
2. They will **not live in the UK for extended periods** through frequent or successive visits, and will not make the UK their **main home**.
3. They are **genuinely seeking entry (or stay)** for a purpose that is permitted under the Visitor route (see `permitted-activities.md`).
4. They will **not undertake any prohibited activity** (see `prohibited-activities.md`).
5. They have **sufficient funds** to cover all reasonable costs of the visit — for themselves and any dependants — without working or accessing public funds, including the return/onward travel cost, any costs relating to dependants, and the cost of any planned paid activity such as private medical treatment (see `financial-requirement.md`).
6. They hold a **valid passport or other travel document**, valid for the whole of the intended stay.
7. Where required, they hold a valid **entry clearance (visa)** for the Standard Visitor route obtained before travel.

Applicants must also not fall foul of general grounds for refusal (e.g. criminality, deception, previous UK immigration breaches, prior overstaying) — those are covered under separate suitability rules in the wider Immigration Rules, not the Visitor-specific eligibility rules described here.

## Age

There is no minimum age. A child can be granted a Standard Visitor visa, but an application on their behalf must be made by a parent or legal guardian, and consent/care arrangements while in the UK may be scrutinised. If a long-term visa (2/5/10 years) is granted to an applicant who is under 18, that visa's validity is capped to expire no later than 6 months after the applicant turns 18.
