# The "Genuine Visitor" Test and Intention to Leave

Every Standard Visitor application is assessed against the "genuine visitor" requirement. This is the single most important qualitative test in the route — an applicant can meet every documentary/financial requirement and still be refused if the caseworker is not satisfied they are a genuine visitor.

## What the caseworker must be satisfied of

- The applicant **will leave the UK** at the end of the visit.
- The applicant will **not live in the UK for extended periods** through frequent or successive visits, and will not effectively make the UK their **main home** ("de facto residence").
- The stated purpose of the visit is **credible** and is one that is actually permitted under the Visitor route.
- The applicant will **not undertake prohibited activities**.

## How caseworkers assess this

Home Office caseworker guidance directs assessment of:

- **Cumulative time spent in the UK** and the **pattern of travel over the previous 12 months** — heavy or repeated use of visit visas can look like de facto residence rather than genuine visiting, even if each individual stay was under 6 months.
- Whether the **information and reasons given for the visit are credible** and correspond to the applicant's personal, family, social and economic circumstances (i.e., does the trip make sense given their life situation, ties, and history).
- **Previous immigration history**, both in the UK and in other countries — e.g. prior removals, prior visa refusals, or breaches of another country's immigration law can weigh against a finding of genuineness, though this is not conclusive on its own.
- Travel history is **not the sole factor** — caseworkers are directed to weigh all relevant information for the specific application, including social/economic ties and any locally held information, not just a travel-pattern algorithm.

## Consequences of failing the test

- A visa already granted **may be cancelled** if an applicant's subsequent travel history shows they are repeatedly living in the UK for extended periods (i.e. behaving as a de facto resident rather than a visitor).
- Even where financial and documentary requirements are otherwise satisfied, failing the genuine visitor test is grounds for **refusal**.

## Practical implication for advisory content

When advising a user on a Standard Visitor application, the strength of "intention to leave" evidence (ties to home country — job, property, family, ongoing studies, return travel booked) and a **credible, specific, and consistent story** about the purpose and itinerary of the trip matter at least as much as the financial evidence itself.
