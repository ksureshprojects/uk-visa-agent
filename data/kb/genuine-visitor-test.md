# The "Genuine Visitor" Test and Intention to Leave

Every Standard Visitor application is assessed against the "genuine visitor" requirement. This is the single most important qualitative test in the route — an applicant can meet every documentary/financial requirement and still be refused if the caseworker is not satisfied they are a genuine visitor.

## Core requirement

Caseworkers must be satisfied that applicants meet all requirements under **Immigration Rules Appendix V, paragraphs V 4.2 to V 4.6** to be considered genuine visitors. In particular, caseworkers must determine whether the applicant:

- "intend[s] only to undertake permitted activities and to leave at the end of their stay"
- will **not** be living in the UK, or making it their home, through frequent successive visits
- will **not** undertake prohibited activities
- has **sufficient funds, maintenance and accommodation**

## Assessing personal circumstances

Caseworker guidance directs decision-makers to consider:

- "previous immigration history, including visits to the UK and other countries"
- "duration of previous visits and whether this was significantly longer than originally stated"
- financial circumstances and family/social/economic background
- "personal and economic ties to their country of residence"
- **cumulative time spent in the UK** and the **pattern of travel over the previous 12 months** — heavy or repeated use of visit visas can look like de facto residence rather than genuine visiting, even if each individual stay was under 6 months
- whether the reasons given for the visit **correspond credibly** to the applicant's personal circumstances

## Travel history analysis

"A pattern of travel that shows the applicant has previously complied with UK immigration law may indicate the applicant is likely to be a genuine visitor." Conversely, removals from other countries or prior immigration violations may weigh against a finding of genuineness — though the guidance is explicit that context matters and this is not conclusive on its own. Travel history is **not the sole factor**: caseworkers must weigh all relevant information for the specific application, including social/economic ties and any locally held information.

## Frequent / successive visits assessment

To determine whether an applicant is effectively residing in the UK through repeat visits, caseworkers review:

- stated visit purposes and intended lengths
- visit frequency over the previous 12 months, including the length of and interval between visits
- whether time spent in the UK **exceeds** time spent in the applicant's home country
- "the links they have with their home country or ordinary country of residence"
- evidence suggesting UK residency (e.g. GP registration, children enrolled in UK schools)

## Red flags for non-genuine intentions

Caseworker concerns may be raised where an applicant:

- has "few or no family and economic ties to their country of residence"
- shows "discrepancies between statements made by the applicant and sponsor"
- provides information that cannot be verified, or that "are not credible"
- has items found in baggage searches that suggest intent to work or reside in the UK rather than visit

## Consequences of failing the test

- Failure to satisfy the genuine visitor requirement results in **refusal** of the application.
- A visa already granted **may be cancelled** if the holder's subsequent travel history shows they are repeatedly living in the UK for extended periods (behaving as a de facto resident rather than a visitor).
- The guidance explicitly notes this determination is **"distinct and separate from suitability"** — a genuine-visitor refusal is a different pathway from a suitability-based refusal (e.g. for criminality or deception).

## Practical implication for advisory content

When advising a user on a Standard Visitor application, the strength of "intention to leave" evidence (ties to home country — job, property, family, ongoing studies, return travel booked) and a **credible, specific, and consistent story** about the purpose and itinerary of the trip matter at least as much as the financial evidence itself.
